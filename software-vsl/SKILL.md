# Software VSL

Create a sales video in which the product demonstration carries the argument. Use Ryan Deiss-style direct-response functions and current software-launch pacing as adaptable influences; do not present the adaptation as an exact canonical Ryan Deiss formula.

## Establish the source of truth

Before scripting, inspect the current offer, implementation, available proof, guarantee, existing page, and any approved prior script. Build a claims ledger for price, limits, speed, results, compatibility, scarcity, and refund terms. Historical handoffs explain decisions but may contain obsolete versions; do not call a script authoritative merely because an old document does.

For a full VSL or script audit, read [references/story-framework.md](references/story-framework.md). For animation, narration generation, editing, or rendering, also read [references/production.md](references/production.md).

## Script around evidence

- Hook with the input-to-output transformation or a recognizable buyer moment.
- Show the product early when the demo is the strongest pattern interrupt.
- Let the viewer see a real input, the relevant steps, and the finished output. Do not use edits that invalidate a speed or ease claim.
- Explain the problem, mechanism, desired use, proof, offer, guarantee, honest boundary, and action in a causal sequence.
- Keep on-screen headline language shorter than narration. Large kinetic lines carry one idea and change at meaningful spoken beats.
- Make narration sound spoken: concrete nouns, contractions, varied cadence, and no vague bridge sentences.
- Keep OTO videos shorter and aware of the preceding purchase. Isolate one new bottleneck and one incremental offer; do not assume another optional offer was accepted.

Treat duration and beat timing as outputs of the argument and delivery. Reference ranges may inform planning, but never pad or cut solely to hit a universal runtime.

When the `no-ai-slop` skill is available, use it as the final voice pass after the story and claims are correct. Otherwise perform the script audit in the story reference and preserve natural fragments, questions, cadence, and the writer's useful rough edges.

## Validate the finished artifact

Review the script alone, then the storyboard, then the rendered video with sound. Check spoken claims, visible text, captions, footage, offer details, animation anchors, audio seams, music ducking, mobile player behavior, reduced motion, and the page around the player. Report measured runtime and remaining limitations.

Do not submit paid generation, publish media, deploy pages, or replace a production master unless the user has authorized that action. Preserve complete provider inputs and job IDs when generation is authorized so an uncertain request can be resumed rather than duplicated.

## Provenance

This skill is based on an in-house comparison of live software-launch VSLs, an existing in-house 12-beat Deiss mapping, and the production of one front-end and two OTO videos. “Ryan Deiss-style” names an influence, not an exact canonical formula. Do not repeat an exact step list or attribution externally without checking a primary source. The production rules come from observed project practice rather than Ryan Deiss.
